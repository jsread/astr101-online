## Thermal Spectra

A hotter object emits light with higher (average) energy
➡︎ Peak wavelength is shorter ➡︎ More blue in color

![Thermal radiation intensity versus wavelength in nanometers, with ultraviolet, visible, and infrared regions marked and curves for different temperatures.](img/thermal-spectra.jpg)

### Comparing thermal spectra
A hotter object emits more intensity at every wavelength,  AND the peak wavelength changes

![](img/samesizevarytemp.gif)


A larger object will emit more intensity at every wavelength, but size doesn’t change the peak wavelength 

![](img/sametempvarysize.gif)

## The Composition of Stars

Meghnad Saha (1893-1956)

Showed how absorption lines depend on temperature and pressure

Cecelia Payne-Gaposchkin 
(1900-1979) 

Used spectral lines under different conditions determine the composition of the sun and stars.


The sun is 
71% hydrogen, 
27% helium, 
and less than 2% all other elements.

“The fact that so many stars have identical spectra is in itself a fact suggesting uniformity of composition; and the success of the theory of thermal ionization in predicting the spectral changes that occur from class to class is a further indication in the same direction.”
Stellar Atmospheres, Cecilia Payne-Gaposchkin, 1925

All stars are mostly hydrogen and helium!
