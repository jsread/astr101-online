## Motion of stars

The rotation and orbit of the Earth gave apparent motions of stars in the sky: 
- the daily revolution of the celestial sphere
- yearly parallax shifts for nearby stars (<arcseconds)

After correcting for Earth’s motion, astronomers can measure a few stars still moving.

This “proper” motion is only noticeable for nearby and fast-moving stars.

Barnard’s Star,
6 light years away

The way a speeding car sounds is due to properties of sound waves. Light waves show the same effect.

Source moving towards us seems to have shorter wavelength:

Pitch sounds higher,   Light looks bluer 

Source moving away seems to have longer wavelength:

Pitch sounds lower,   Light looks redder



### Stationary source, stationary observer
![](img/doppler1.gif)

Circles mark the “crests” of outgoing waves
Wavelength: distance between crests

### Moving source
![](img/doppler2.gif)

Source moves to right.

Observer on left side: Wavelengths appear longer:
redshift

Observer on right side: Wavelengths appear shorter:
blueshift

Observer looking from top or bottom side: Wavelengths do not change

### Moving observer, stationary source?
Same observed shifts in wavelength/frequency: doppler effect comes from relative motion

## Doppler and Spectra

- Absorption and emission spectra have lines at precisely predicted wavelengths.

- Doppler shift shifts the wavelength of the lines in the spectra.

- Astronomers use the Doppler Effect to learn about radial motions (toward or away from the observer, along the line of sight) of light sources (stars, galaxies). 

- It works for objects that are so far away that we see no change in their position in the sky!

### Example: hydrogen

Top: not moving
Middle: Moving toward you at 3000 km/s
Bottom: Moving toward you at 30,000 km/s

### Doppler and color

Important note:  most doppler shifts are too tiny to significantly change the color of an object!

![A red sticker that says "If this sticker is blue, then you are driving too fast."](img/aps-driving-sticker.jpg)
